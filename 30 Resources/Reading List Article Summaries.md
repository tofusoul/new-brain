## 📰 Articles

### Wisdom of Crowds (2 articles)
- **BBC: Wisdom of Crowds** — Explores when crowd wisdom works and when it fails. Galton's 1907 ox-weight experiment showed group averages can be scarily accurate, but social influence and groupthink can bias crowds badly. Independence of judgment is key.
- **MIT Tech Review: Wisdom of the Confident** — Neurobiologists found that identifying "confident" individuals (less influenced by others' opinions) and using their estimates alone produces better results than full crowd averages. Basically: ignore the followers, listen to the independent thinkers.

### History & Politics
- **Montreal Protocol (Earth Day)** — How the world successfully phased out ozone-depleting CFCs in the 1980s. Shows international cooperation *can* work on environmental crises, though climate change is a harder problem (it touches every part of the economy, not just a few chemicals).
- **Texas Is the Future of America (NYTimes)** — Op-ed arguing Texas, not California, is the real bellwether for America's future. Fast-growing, already majority-minority, rapidly urbanizing — its political battles foreshadow the nation's.
- **Chomsky Interview (Jacobin)** — Wide-ranging 2019 interview covering climate change, US imperialism, Venezuela, Iran. Chomsky argues the Green New Deal is sound economics but faces enormous political obstacles. Characteristically doom-laden but urgent.
- **Rise of the Useless Class (TED/Yuval Noah Harari)** — Harari argues AI will create a new "useless class" of people whose labour isn't needed. Challenges the assumption that new jobs always replace old ones. Organisms are just algorithms, silicon or carbon — nothing guarantees humans stay valuable.
- **14 Rules for Eternal Fascism (Overland)** — Analyses Jordan Peterson through the lens of Eco's "14 features of fascism." Written after working with teenagers on a devised theatre piece. Connects Peterson's Hero's Journey rhetoric to fascist aesthetics.
- **The Intellectual We Deserve (Current Affairs)** — Extended takedown of Jordan Peterson as a thinker. Argues he combines obvious truisms with deliberate obscurantism to appear profound. Very long (12,000+ words) and thorough.
- **Trade War (Forbes)** — 2018 piece on how US-China trade war was rearranging supply chains and hurting China's economy. A-shares down 30%, manufacturers vacating for other Asian countries. Now outdated by subsequent developments.
- **Budgeting Kills Agile (Forbes)** — How traditional annual budgeting is fundamentally incompatible with Agile. Budgets are rigid, forward-looking contracts; Agile is iterative and adaptive. The "Beyond Budgeting" movement proposes rolling forecasts and adaptive resource allocation.
- **How McKinsey Destroyed the Middle Class (The Atlantic)** — Argues management consulting (McKinsey especially) hollowed out middle management, eliminated organization men, and drove extreme inequality. Consultants taught companies to treat workers as costs to minimize rather than stakeholders to develop.
- **Friendster Autopsy (Wired)** — Academic study of why Friendster died. Not user count — it still had millions. The problem was weak connections between users (low "K-core density"). When the 2009 redesign hit, the loosely-connected outer users left first, cascading inward. Lesson: networks need strong ties to survive.
- **HBR: The New New Product Development Game (1986)** — The classic paper that inspired Scrum/Agile. Takeuchi & Nonaka argue the old sequential "relay race" product development is too slow. Instead, use cross-functional teams that pass the ball like rugby — overlapping phases, self-organizing, multilearning. This is basically the founding document of modern agile.
- **Coronavirus vs Spanish Flu (NYTimes)** — Compares COVID-19 to 1918 flu. Key differences: Spanish flu killed mostly 20-40 year olds via cytokine storm; COVID disproportionately kills elderly. Medicine, hygiene, and surveillance are vastly better now.
- **Bob Jones v Renae Maihi (The Spinoff)** — NZ High Court defamation case where Bob Jones sued a Māori woman for calling his column racist. He withdrew the case during cross-examination. Vivid courtroom reporting of Jones making a fool of himself.
- **Living the Moral High Life (The Spinoff)** — A week of consuming only from social enterprises in Auckland. Ate at Everybody Eats (pay-what-you-can restaurant), used Eat My Lunch (buy one, donate one). Found social enterprises hard to identify and a fragmented ecosystem in NZ.

### Programming & Tech
- **Level Up Your Regex Game (Medium)** — Practical JavaScript regex tutorial covering character classes, quantifiers, groups, anchors, flags, lookahead/lookbehind. Good intermediate reference. About 2,000 words.
- **Web Design in 4 Minutes (jgthms.com)** — Brilliant, concise CSS tutorial. Covers the essentials: centering content, font families, spacing, colour/contrast, balance, primary colour, secondary colours, gradients, shadows, fonts. The CSS framework Bulma was born from this. Worth keeping.
- **What Is Code? (Bloomberg)** — Legendary 38,000-word explainer of what code is, written for non-programmers. Told as a narrative about a manager trying to understand software. Covers history, culture, economics of software development. A masterpiece of explanatory writing.
- **Functors, Applicatives, Monads in Pictures (Elm)** — Visual guide to functional programming concepts using Elm. Translated from the famous Haskell version. Uses pictures/diagrams to explain containers, mapping, applying, and binding. Good if you're learning FP.
- **How to Think About Monads (Gist)** — Text-based explanation of monads. Starts from pure function composition, shows how side effects break it, then shows how monads restore composability. Clearer than the pictures version for some people.
- **Scuttlebutt** — Decentralised social network built by NZ developer Dominic Tarr. Works offline, syncs peer-to-peer when you meet people (or via USB). Anti-corporate, pro-privacy, local-first. The tech is interesting but the network is small.
- **HTTP Overview (MDN)** — Standard Mozilla reference doc. Covers HTTP as client-server protocol, components (proxies, caches), HTTP/2, evolution from HTTP/0.9 to HTTP/3. Reference material, not really a "read cover to cover" article.
- **Kafka Parables** — Collection of Kafka's short parables and paradoxes: "Before the Law," "An Imperial Message," "The Tower of Babel," "The Coming of the Messiah," "Abraham," etc. Literary/philosophical. Dense and rewarding.

### Regenerative Agriculture (2 articles)
- **Regenerative Organic Agriculture (Rodale Institute)** — Short explainer. "Regenerative" goes beyond "sustainable" — it actively improves soil health, not just maintains it. Prioritises soil health, animal welfare, and worker fairness. Led to the Regenerative Organic Certified standard.
- **Redirecting Agricultural Subsidies (WRI)** — Governments spend $600B/year on agricultural subsidies, but only 5% supports conservation. Redirecting even a portion could massively improve food security and reduce emissions. Pure income support is 70% of spending.

### Career & Business
- **China Careers (80,000 Hours)** — Career guide for becoming a China-West specialist focused on global catastrophic risks (AI safety, biosecurity). Argues coordination between China and West is crucial but lacking. Very long (8,000+ words) and specific to effective altruism career planning.
- **Multi-Millionaire Habits (Thrive Global/Dr. Benjamin Hardy)** — Self-help listicle: invest 10% of income in yourself, stop watching TV, stop caring what others think, read more, write daily, set goals, find mentors, etc. Very standard self-improvement advice.
- **Early Retirement Tips (CNBC)** — Retired-at-35 couple's advice on what to stop spending money on: eating out ($9K/year!), cable TV, new cars, expensive clothes, gadgets, alcohol. Practical FIRE movement content.
- **Cover Letter Guide (freeCodeCamp)** — Detailed cover letter writing guide with framework: Problem → Agitate → Solution → Call to action. Includes template. Useful if you're job hunting.

### ADHD & Mind Issues (9 articles — from ADDitude Magazine)
- **Brain Training & Neurofeedback for ADHD** — FAQ on whether brain training apps and neurofeedback actually work for ADHD. Short answer: mixed evidence. Working memory training has some support. Lumosity-type apps are mostly marketing. Neurofeedback shows promise but is expensive and inconsistent.
- **CBT for ADHD** — Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for ADHD adults. Addresses "cognitive distortions" (negative self-talk patterns built up from years of setbacks). CBT is about restructuring automatic negative thoughts like "I never do anything right." Good evidence base.
- **Decision Making with ADHD** — ADHDers are great at "hot decisions" (crisis situations — dopamine fires up) but terrible at "cold decisions" (deliberate, information-heavy choices). Explains executive function scaffolding and how to compensate.
- **Procrastination Stories** — Personal essay about ADHD-related procrastination. Found a folder of letters he never replied to, including one to his deceased grandmother. "20 years of deep guilt." Practical advice: draw a line, start leaving notes, follow through.
- **Anxiety in Adults with ADHD** — Dr. Ned Hallowell explains the overlap between ADHD and anxiety (25-40% comorbidity). ADHD creates plenty to worry about. Anxiety can actually be stimulating for ADHD brains seeking mental focus. Treatments discussed.
- **Analysis Paralysis (13 tips)** — Slideshow on beating ADHD decision paralysis. Tips include: limit options to 3, use "good enough" threshold, set time limits, flip a coin (you'll often know your real preference by how you feel about the coin result).
- **Sleep Help for ADHD Adults** — Reasons ADHDers can't sleep: racing brain, medication timing, circadian rhythm disruption. Tips: do your own sleep study, try stimulant before bed (paradoxically helps some people), sleep hygiene basics, consistent schedule.
- **Productivity Tips for ADHD Brains** — Practical workplace tips: be realistic about time (everything takes 2x longer), forget perfect, prep your environment, use positive affirmations ("I can and I will"), visible deadlines, body doubling, work in bursts.
- **To-Do List Delegation** — Why ADHDers struggle to delegate: can't give clear instructions, don't feel entitled to ask. Solution: take time to map out the task steps, make a diagram, then ask the person for feedback on your instructions before handing it off.

### Society & Culture
- **Intentional Communities Fail (Aeon)** — Long essay on why intentional communities (communes, eco-villages) fail at about the same rate as start-ups. Explores Tamera in Portugal. Core problem: reproducing the same interpersonal dynamics (ego, power, jealousy) they're trying to escape.
- **Rochdale Principles (Wikipedia)** — The 7 cooperative principles (voluntary membership, democratic control, member economic participation, autonomy, education, cooperation between co-ops, concern for community). Originated in 1844 Rochdale, UK.
- **Wonder From Home (Atlas Obscura)** — This was actually a *series* page, not a single article. It's Atlas Obscura's collection of virtual experiences and armchair travel content. Not really a single thing to "read."
- **Bob Jones (The Spinoff)** — (covered above)
- **Moral High Life (The Spinoff)** — (covered above)
- **Chinese Philosophy & Mental Health (Psyche)** — Argues ancient Chinese philosophy recognised mental illness as communal, not just individual. The *Huangdi Neijing* (4th century BCE) described disorders caused by community dysfunction, not just personal pathology. Modern Western psychiatry over-emphasises the individual.
